Overview

What is CrewSync?

CrewSync is a scheduling and coordination app for flight crews. It's built to handle pilots from any airline or flight operation — corporate, regional, charter, or mixed — all in one place. Each pilot uploads their own schedule, and the app handles the rest: tracking where everyone is, finding overlaps, and surfacing meetup opportunities automatically.

The core idea: stop texting "hey, are you in ORD this week?" and let the app tell you. Every pilot's schedule — corporate flights, airline trips, reserve windows, days off — feeds into a shared picture that the whole crew can see.

What it does

  • Calendar Grid — Your month at a glance, color-coded by day type, with city names on each flight leg
  • List View — A chronological feed of all your flights and events
  • Route Map — Your flight routes plotted on a live map, plus where the whole crew is today
  • Location Overlap — Automatically finds when two pilots will be in the same city at the same time
  • Crew Intel — A shared knowledge base of hotels, food, activities, and tips for airports the crew has visited
  • Notifications — Push alerts when a crossing is detected after a schedule upload

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't connect to airline systems directly — schedules are uploaded manually by each pilot
  • It doesn't replace your airline's scheduling app — it's a crew coordination layer on top
  • It's read-only for viewers (guests with view-only access)

Getting Started

Access to CrewSync is by personal link. Your link is unique to you — it signs you in automatically without a password.

Opening the app

Open your personal link in a browser. You'll land directly in the app signed in as yourself. Bookmark it, or better yet, install it to your home screen so it behaves like a native app.

iOS users: Add the app to your Home Screen via Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen before enabling notifications. Push notifications only work from the installed PWA, not from the browser tab.

What you see on first load

The Calendar Grid opens by default, showing the current month for the first pilot in the roster. If you're signed in as yourself, the app loads your schedule automatically. Use the pilot selector (sidebar on desktop, scroll bar on mobile) to switch between crew members.

Navigating between views

On desktop, use the left sidebar. On mobile, use the bottom navigation bar:

  • Grid — Monthly calendar
  • List — Chronological flight list
  • Map — Route and crew map
  • Overlap — Location crossings
  • Upload — Upload your schedule (mobile)

Views

Calendar Grid

The calendar shows a full month of scheduled activity in a color-coded grid. Each day is colored based on the highest-priority event on that day.

Day color codes

  • Blue — Flying day. One or more flights are scheduled.
  • Amber — Reserve / on call. Airline pilot has a reserve window on this day.
  • Yellow — Away layover. Overnight at a non-home airport.
  • Red — Hard day off. Scheduled unavailable day.
  • Teal — Vacation block.
  • Gray — Open day. No events scheduled. For corporate pilots this typically means available; for airline pilots it means a day off.

Tapping a day

Tap any day to open the detail sheet. It shows every event for that day: flight routes with city/state names, tail numbers and block times, reserve windows, hard-off reasons, and layover locations. City and state appear below each airport pair when the airport is in the database — useful for airports you don't recognize by code. Active flights (currently airborne) are highlighted.

Navigating months

Use the ‹ and › arrows to move between months. The app loads schedule data for whichever pilot is currently selected.

Switching pilots

On desktop: pilot buttons in the left sidebar. On mobile: the scrollable pill bar below the month header. Switching pilots loads that person's schedule for the current month.

Views

List View

The list view shows all events for the selected pilot in chronological order. Unlike the calendar grid, it doesn't group by week — it's a flat, scrollable feed of flights, layovers, days off, and reserve windows.

What's shown

  • Flights — Route (e.g. STL → LGA) with city names in smaller text below when both airports are recognized, departure and arrival times in local airport time, flight number, tail, block time
  • Ground transport — Van or bus legs between airports (airline ops)
  • Reserve — On-call windows with start and end times
  • Hard off — Scheduled days off
  • Vacation — Vacation blocks
  • Away — Multi-day layovers away from base

Deadhead flights

Deadhead (DH) flights are shown with a DH badge. These are positioning flights where the pilot is a passenger, not flying the aircraft.

Live flights

Flights that are currently airborne are highlighted with a green pulse indicator. The app compares the current time against departure and arrival times to determine live status.

Views

Route Map

The map has three modes, switchable via the sub-menu on desktop or the mode bar on mobile.

My Routes

Shows the selected pilot's flight routes for the current month as arcs on the map. Each arc connects a departure airport to an arrival airport. Color corresponds to the pilot's crew color. Clicking an arc shows the flight details.

All Crew — Day

Shows where every crew member is on a specific day. Location pins indicate each pilot's current airport — either where they're based for the day, or where they landed most recently. Use the date navigator to step forward or backward by day.

This is the most useful mode for planning: you can quickly see who's in what city on any given day.

Pins use each pilot's crew color. The pin label shows the airport code. If a pilot is in the air, their pin shows their most recent departure or destination.

All Crew — Month

Shows all flight routes for all pilots for the current month simultaneously. Good for getting a big-picture sense of where the crew is flying this month.

Weather Radar

The radar toggle (top-right of the map) overlays live precipitation radar. Useful for checking weather along a route.

Location Overlap

This is the core coordination feature. The app automatically computes when any two pilots will be in the same city at the same time — based purely on their uploaded schedules. No manual input required.

How it works

For each pilot, the app builds a timeline of where they'll be on the ground and for how long. It then compares every pair of pilots and finds windows where they're at the same location (or nearby locations) simultaneously. These are called crossings.

The overlap view is always computed fresh from the latest schedule data. Hit Refresh to recompute after an upload.

Crossing tiers

Not all crossings are equal. The app classifies them into tiers:

  • Tier 1 — City Meetup — Both pilots are in the same metro area. SFO, OAK, SJC, APC are all treated as "Bay Area." STL, SUS, CPS are all treated as "St. Louis." These are real, plannable meetups.
  • Tier 2 — Same Region — Airports within roughly 50 miles. Still likely plannable with a short drive.
  • Tier 3 — Also Nearby — Geographic coincidences. Shown in a collapsed section at the bottom. Worth knowing but less actionable.

Reading a crossing card

Each card shows:

  • The two pilots involved and their respective airports
  • Each pilot's arrival and departure times at that location
  • The Together stripe at the bottom — the direct overlap window when both pilots are simultaneously on the ground
  • Duration of the overlap

All times are shown in the local timezone of the meeting airport — not your browser's timezone.

Opening the detail modal

Tap any crossing card to open the detail view. It shows a full breakdown: exactly when each pilot arrives and departs, the together window with timezone labels, and the distance between airports if they're at different locations in the same metro area.

Your crossings only

The crossings view only shows crossings that involve you. You won't see crossings between two other pilots — the view is scoped to what's relevant to you.

Reserve days count

Reserve windows are included in the crossing computation. If a pilot is on reserve at SFO from 6 AM to 9 PM, they're considered present at SFO for that entire window — even though they might not be flying.

Off Days tab

The second tab in the overlap view shows days where multiple pilots have days off simultaneously. Useful for spotting windows where the crew could plan something together.

Views

Crew Intel

Crew Intel is a shared knowledge base for the crew. Any pilot can add hotels, food spots, activities, and tips for airports they've visited — and everyone benefits. It's the crew's collective answer to "where should I stay in ORD?" or "is there anything good to eat near TEB?"

Browsing airports

The default view lists every airport with intel as a card. Each card shows the airport code, city name, and a count of entries by category. Use the search bar at the top to filter by airport code or city name. Tap any card to open the detail view for that airport.

Detail view

The detail view shows all entries for a single airport. Use the category pills at the top to filter — tap All, Hotels, Food, Activities, or Tips. Tap any entry to expand the full description.

Intel Map

Switch to the Map tab to see all intel locations plotted as thumbtack pins on a world map. Pins are color-coded by category:

  • Blue — Hotels
  • Amber — Food
  • Green — Activities
  • Purple — Tips
  • Red — Mixed (multiple categories at one airport)

Tap any pin to see a summary of what intel exists at that airport. A legend in the bottom-left corner shows the color key. The map fits all pins in view on load.

Categories

  • Hotel — Where to stay. Crew-friendly spots, quiet rooms, easy transport to the airport.
  • Food — Good eats nearby. Late-night options, delivery that works, places worth the walk.
  • Activity — Things to do on a long layover. Gyms, trails, sights if you have time.
  • Tip — Anything else. Parking, transport tricks, FBO notes, heads-up about the hotel van — stuff that doesn't fit elsewhere.

Adding an entry

Tap + Add in the top right. Enter the airport code, pick a category, write a short title, and optionally add a longer description. Your entry appears immediately for the whole crew.

Editing and deleting

You can edit or delete any entry you added. Tap the pencil icon to edit — you can update the airport code, category, title, or description. Tap the trash icon to delete. Admin accounts can edit or delete any entry in the database.

City and state names are shown on each airport card and in the detail view header — pulled from the same global airport database the rest of the app uses, so they work for international airports too.

Managing Data

Uploading Schedules

Each pilot is responsible for uploading their own schedule. The app supports several file formats depending on the airline or scheduling system.

How to upload

Desktop: Use the upload section at the bottom of the left sidebar. Select the pilot from the dropdown (if you're an admin), then click "Upload Schedule" and choose your file.

Mobile: Tap the Upload button in the bottom nav bar. A sheet slides up with your upload options.

What happens after upload

  • The file is parsed on the server and converted into individual flight segments
  • Segments are stored in the database under your pilot account
  • The app immediately reloads your schedule data
  • Crossing notifications are automatically computed and sent to affected pilots
Uploading a new schedule doesn't delete old data. It adds or updates segments for the period covered by the new file. If a segment already exists with the same flight details, it's updated in place.

Direct sync (corporate ops)

Pilots whose schedules are managed in a supported scheduling platform can sync directly without uploading a file. If your account is configured for direct sync, a sync button appears in the upload section. Tap it to pull your latest schedule automatically.

Supported formats

See the Schedule Formats section for a breakdown by airline.

Managing Data

Adding Flights Manually

If a flight isn't in your uploaded schedule — a personal trip, a charter, a positioning leg — you can add it manually.

How to add

Click or tap the + Add Flight button (top-right on desktop, + on mobile). Fill in the departure airport, arrival airport, date, and times. Tail number and flight number are optional.

Manually added vs. uploaded

Manually added flights are stored separately from uploaded schedule data. They show up in the calendar, list view, map, and crossing computation just like any other flight. They won't be overwritten if you re-upload your schedule.

Editing or deleting

Tap a manually added flight in the list view or day detail sheet to edit or delete it. Uploaded segments cannot be individually edited — re-upload the schedule to update them.

Notifications

CrewSync can send push notifications when a new crossing is detected — so you find out about a potential meetup the moment a schedule is uploaded, without having to check the app.

Enabling notifications

When you first open the app as a signed-in pilot, a prompt appears in the sidebar asking to enable crossing alerts. Click Enable and allow the browser permission dialog that appears.

You can also enable or disable notifications any time from your Profile (tap your name/avatar in the top corner). The Notifications section shows the current status and lets you toggle it on or off for the current device.

iPhone users: Notifications only work when the app is installed as a PWA (Add to Home Screen). If you're using it in Safari as a regular webpage, push notifications won't arrive. Once installed from your home screen, it works exactly like a native app notification.

What triggers a notification

  • Any pilot uploads a schedule that creates a new crossing with you
  • The same crossing won't trigger a second notification — deduplication prevents repeat alerts for the same event

What the notification looks like

The notification appears as a standard OS push notification with the app icon. It shows who you'll be crossing with, the airport or city, the time window, and the date. Tapping it opens the app directly to the Crossings view.

The bell icon

The bell icon in the top-right of the app header shows your unread notification count. Tapping it opens a panel with your recent notifications. Opening the Crossings view marks all notifications as read and clears the home screen badge.

Multiple devices

If you use the app on both your iPhone and a desktop browser, you can enable notifications on each device separately. Both will receive push alerts independently.

Account

Your Personal Link

CrewSync doesn't use passwords. Your personal link is a unique URL that signs you in automatically. It looks like:

https://yourapp.com/app?u=abc123xyz456

The token at the end (abc123xyz456) is your credential. Keep it to yourself — anyone with your link can view and upload as you.

Finding your link

Tap your name or avatar to open the Profile sheet. Your personal link is shown there with a Copy button.

iOS PWA and link persistence

When you install the app to your home screen on iOS, Safari strips the ?u= token from the URL. The app handles this automatically — it saves your token to local storage on your first visit, so opening from the home screen still signs you in.

Sharing with viewers

View-only guests have their own links with read-only access. They can see all schedules but can't upload or add flights. Admins can generate viewer links from the Manage Users panel.

Installing the App

CrewSync is a Progressive Web App (PWA) — it can be installed to your home screen and behaves like a native app. This is recommended for all crew members, especially for push notifications.

iPhone / iPad (Safari)

  • Open your personal link in Safari (not Chrome or Firefox)
  • Tap the Share button ⎙ at the bottom of the screen
  • Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen
  • Tap Add in the top-right corner
  • Open the app from your home screen going forward
Must use Safari. Chrome and Firefox on iOS cannot install PWAs to the home screen.

Mac (Safari or Chrome)

In Chrome: click the install icon in the address bar (looks like a screen with a down arrow). In Safari: File → Add to Dock.

Desktop Chrome / Edge

Click the install icon in the address bar (right side). The app opens in its own window without browser chrome.

Android (Chrome)

Chrome shows an "Add to Home Screen" banner automatically, or use the three-dot menu → Add to Home Screen.

Schedule Formats

CrewSync is built to handle schedules from virtually any airline or flight operation. Each pilot's format is detected automatically on first upload and remembered from then on. Pilots from different airlines with different scheduling systems can all coexist in the same crew.

Supported formats

Format Used by How to export
ICS / Calendar Corporate ops, scheduling platforms, RosterBuster Export from your scheduling platform as .ics
CSV (SkyWest) SkyWest Airlines Export from CrewPortal as CSV
CSV (Republic) Republic Airways Export from CrewPortal as CSV
PDF (Netline) GoJet, and other Netline carriers Download monthly PDF from Netline
VCS (AIMS) AIMS-based operations Export roster from AIMS as .vcs
Adding a new airline? If your scheduling system isn't listed, contact the admin. The app is designed to be extended — new parsers can be added without changing anything for existing pilots.

Time zones in uploaded files

Different formats store times differently, and the app handles both automatically:

  • UTC-based (ICS, direct sync) — Times have a Z suffix indicating UTC. The app converts to local airport time for display and uses true UTC for crossing computation.
  • Local-time-based (CSV, PDF) — Times are stored in the local time of the departure or arrival airport with no timezone indicator. The app uses a global airport database to determine the correct timezone for each airport, ensuring crossing windows are computed accurately even across timezone boundaries.

Direct sync (corporate ops)

For pilots whose accounts are configured for direct sync, the app can pull the current schedule without uploading a file. A sync button appears in the upload section and pulls the latest schedule on demand.

FAQ

Why are my times showing in the wrong timezone?
The app always displays times in the local timezone of the relevant airport. A flight arriving at SFO shows its arrival time in Pacific time, even if you're viewing it from a different timezone. This is intentional — you want to know what time it is at the airport, not at your current location.
How often does the schedule data update?
Data only updates when a pilot uploads a new schedule file or triggers a direct sync. There's no automatic background sync — it depends on each pilot uploading when their schedule changes (new bid, trip swap, etc.). Direct sync can be triggered manually with one tap for configured accounts.
What airports count as the same city for crossings?
The app uses metro groups — clusters of airports treated as the same city for crossing purposes. Key ones:
  • Bay Area: SFO, OAK, SJC, STS, SQL, APC
  • St. Louis: STL, SUS, CPS
  • New York: JFK, LGA, EWR, HPN, TEB
  • Los Angeles: LAX, BUR, LGB, SNA, ONT, VNY, SMO
  • Chicago: ORD, MDW, PWK, DPA
Airports within ~50 miles of each other that aren't in the same metro group are treated as Tier 2 crossings.
Why doesn't a crossing show up that I know should be there?
A few things to check: (1) Make sure both pilots have uploaded their current schedule. (2) Hit Refresh on the Crossings page to recompute. (3) If one pilot's schedule doesn't include that trip yet (trip swap, open time pickup), the app won't know about it. (4) Check that the airports involved are in the same metro group or within 50 miles.
Can I see another pilot's full schedule?
Yes. All crew schedules are visible to all crew members. Use the pilot selector to switch to any pilot and browse their calendar, list view, or map. This is by design — the whole point is coordination, which requires visibility.
I got a notification but the crossing is already past — what happened?
This can happen if a schedule was uploaded for a period that includes past dates, or if your device was offline when the push was sent and delivered it late. The notification history (bell icon) always reflects when the notification was originally generated, not when it was delivered.
How do I get a new personal link if mine stops working?
Contact your administrator. They can regenerate your link from the admin panel. Your old link will stop working immediately when a new one is issued, so make sure to update your bookmark.
