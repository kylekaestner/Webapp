How Location Overlap Works

Location Overlap shows your crossings with the rest of the crew — switch pilots at the top to see a different person's view. Only crossings involving the selected pilot are shown.

Overlap Types

Overnight Both pilots at the same airport for 5+ hours or overnight — plenty of time to meet up.
Meetup Same airport, same time — shorter window but still worth a coffee at the gate.
Passing Through Same airport, but one pilot is on a tight connection. Quick hello if timing works out.
City Meetup Different airports in the same metro, but both have real overnight time — could meet in the city.
Home Turf One pilot is overnighting in another crew member's home city.
Near Miss Same location within 3 hours of each other but no direct overlap — close but not quite.

Metro Areas

Airports in the same city are grouped — JFK, LGA, EWR, and TEB all count as "New York Area." The specific airports are shown in smaller text below the city name.

Common Off Days

Collapsed section at the top showing upcoming days where 2+ selected crew are free. Tap the header to expand. For Kyle, only confirmed Hard Day Offs count — on-call days are treated as potentially working. Days where all selected pilots are free show a gold left border and ✦ ALL OFF badge. Use the pilot chips inside the section to filter down to just the people you care about — tap any chip to toggle them on or off. Who appears is also controlled by your Your Crew visibility setting — pilots you've hidden there won't show up here. If a pilot's schedule data hasn't been loaded yet, a ⚠ warning appears at the top of the list noting who's missing, so you know the results may be incomplete.

Also Nearby

Geographic coincidences — airports that are close but a meetup isn't realistic (transiting, different airports across town). Tap a row to expand for detail.

Accuracy

Calculated from scheduled ground time. Add commute and personal flights manually for best results — especially for positioning to base before a trip.

Crew Intel

Crew Intel is a shared knowledge base for the crew. Any pilot can add hotels, food spots, activities, and general tips for airports they've been to — and everyone benefits.

Categories

Hotel Where to stay — crew-friendly spots, quiet rooms, easy transport to the airport.
Food Good eats nearby — late-night options, delivery that actually works, places worth the walk.
Activity Things to do on a long layover — gyms, trails, things worth seeing if you have time.
Tip Anything else — parking, transport tricks, heads-up about the FBO, stuff that doesn't fit elsewhere.

Browsing

The main screen has two views. Airports (default) lists every location with intel as a card, showing category counts and city name. Tap a card to open the detail view — use the category pills to filter by type, and tap any entry to expand it. Use the search bar to filter by airport code or city. Switch to the Map tab to see all intel locations as pins on a world map.

Intel Map

The Map tab plots every airport with intel as a color-coded thumbtack pin. Blue = hotels, amber = food, green = activities, purple = tips. Airports with intel in multiple categories show a red pin. Tap any pin to see a summary of what's there. A legend in the bottom-left shows the color key.

Adding an Entry

Tap + Add in the top right. Pick the airport, select a category, give it a short title, and optionally add a description. Your entry is visible to the whole crew immediately.

Editing & Deleting

You can edit or delete your own entries, including changing the airport code. Admin can manage all entries. Tap the pencil or trash icons that appear on entries you own.

How the Calendar Works

The calendar shows a full month of scheduled flights, days off, and overnights in a color-coded grid. Tap any day to see the full detail sheet.

Color Coding

Flying — active flight leg (work or revenue)
Commute — commute leg to or from base
Personal — personal or leisure flight
Hard Off / Away — scheduled day off or away block
Green outline — flight is currently live (airborne)
DH badge — deadhead leg (riding as passenger)

Navigation

Use Prev / Next to change months. The current day is highlighted. Tap any cell to open a detail sheet with full flight info for that day — routes include city and state names below each airport pair when available, so you don't need to know every code by heart. The header shows ▶ IN THE AIR in green if a flight is currently airborne, and each live flight card shows a green border and ▶ LIVE badge.

Adding Flights

Use the + Add Flight button to manually add Work, Deadhead, Commute, or Personal flights. For DH, commute, and personal flights, entering a flight number auto-fills times from OpenSky.

Syncing Schedules

Kyle syncs automatically via Schedaero. Drew syncs via RosterBuster ICS. Adam, Sam, and Logan upload a CSV from their airline app. Use the Upload tab on mobile. Uploading a new month's schedule never deletes existing data — flights are added or updated in place. If the same flight already exists it gets updated; everything else stays untouched, so uploading June won't wipe out May.

How the List View Works

The list view shows every flight and event in chronological order — one row per leg. It's the best way to see exact block times, overnight durations, and flight details at a glance.

Reading Each Row

Airport pair — departure → arrival with local times shown. City and state appear in smaller text below when both airports resolve to a known city.

Block time — actual block-to-block flight duration in hours and minutes

Overnight badge — shown between legs when ground time exceeds a few hours, includes duration

Flight number / tail — shown when available from the schedule file

Color Coding

Same colors as the calendar: blue for work flights, amber for commutes, violet for personal, gray for off/away, with a green pulse on any live flight.

Editing Flights

Manually added flights show an Edit and Delete button on each row. Synced schedule flights cannot be edited individually — re-sync to update them.

Overnight vs. Home

Overnight durations are shown when the pilot overnights away from home base. If the gap is more than 30 hours and the destination is home, no overnight tag is shown — the pilot is assumed to have commuted home.

How the Map Works

The map plots flight routes as great-circle arcs and shows live ADS-B positions when aircraft are airborne. Switch modes using the tabs at the top.

Map Modes

My Routes Your full month of routes — all legs plotted as arcs. Tap any arc to see all flights on that route with departure and arrival times (including timezone).
All Crew — Day Everyone's flights on a single day. Tap any arc to see the pilot, route, block time, and times with timezone — plus status: ▶ In the air now, ◷ Departs in…, or ✓ Completed. Completed flights are shown at 25% opacity so upcoming and live legs stand out. Use the date arrows to step forward and back.
All Crew — Month All crew routes for the month overlaid. Tap any arc to see whose flight it is and how many times they flew that route.

Here Now

The purple pulsing dot shows where a pilot currently is. Tap it to see whether they're home or on an overnight, and which airport.

Live ADS-B

When a flight is airborne, the map polls ADS-B Exchange every 8 seconds. A dashed arc shows the remaining path to destination; the solid colored trail behind is the actual GPS track. The route arc and its tap target stay aligned with the live position, so popups always work mid-flight.

Legend & Filters

In All Crew modes, the legend shows each pilot's color — only pilots you've enabled under Your Crew appear here. Tap a legend entry to temporarily toggle that pilot's routes on or off without changing your crew settings. Use the Flown / Upcoming filter to show only past or future legs.

Dots vs. Arcs

A colored dot at an airport means the pilot is on the ground there. An arc with an airplane icon means they're airborne along that route right now.
